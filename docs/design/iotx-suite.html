Will this be sellable?

Yes

Yes, if it is sold as "your company's own software, plus the tools your team already needs" to mid-sized industrial companies like Supreme Steel and Crystal Arc. It is a harder sell as a general office suite that competes head-on with Google Workspace and Microsoft 365.

Why companies will pay

  • They already pay IOTx for custom software. Today each change waits for your developers. With the suite, they make small dashboards and trackers themselves the same day, and call IOTx for the big systems.
  • Spreadsheets and WhatsApp run most of their operations. Turning a sheet into a proper app (with logins, approvals and a dashboard) is the most common need, and the builder does exactly that.
  • One login, one bill, one admin. Owners like seeing every tool, every app and every person in one place, with control over who sees what.
  • The software never runs out. A client starts with a small dashboard and grows to a Supreme Steel-level system without changing products.

Zoho One is the closest comparison: a suite plus a low-code builder. The edge for IOTx is AI building in plain language, industry templates taken from real client projects, and a team that builds the large systems alongside the client.

What has to be true

Do

  • Lead with the builder, AI chat and the internal app store.
  • Connect the client's existing Google or Microsoft mail and calendar at first, instead of asking them to switch.
  • Ship industry templates (inventory, dispatch, quality checks, orders) from day one.
  • Sell the Transformation service alongside every suite.

Watch out for

  • Thirteen tools is a lot to make good. Each one people use daily (mail, sheets) is judged against Google and Microsoft.
  • Companies won't move their email in month one. Make switching optional.
  • Admins need to feel safe: clear roles, audit logs, and their data kept in their organization.

A simple way to price it

  • Per person per month for the suite (every employee who signs in).
  • Build credits included each month for making apps with AI, with top-ups.
  • Transformation projects priced separately, as IOTx does today.

The two offers

Offer 1

The configured product (IOTx Suite)

The client signs up, IOTx switches on the modules they bought, and their people start working on day one: AI chat, the builder, the app store and the everyday tools. The client builds their own dashboards and apps whenever they need them.

Offer 2

Transformation

For systems the size of Supreme Steel's, the IOTx team designs and builds a custom app with the client, inside their suite. When it's done, it appears in their app store like any other app, and the client can keep extending it themselves.

Who uses it

Owner and admins

Set up the organization, departments and roles. Decide which modules and apps each role sees. See usage and billing.

Department heads

Approve apps for their team, share dashboards, and ask IOTx for larger builds.

Builders

The people who like making things, often ops or finance. They build apps by chatting and connect data from sheets or databases.

Everyone else

Sign in, see only the tools and apps they're given, and get their work done. Ask the AI anything about the company.

IOTx team

Onboards the client, trains builders, and delivers Transformation projects.

How it's arranged

The organization

Each client is one organization, for example Crystal Arc. Inside it are departments (Sales, Production, Dispatch, Accounts, HR) and people, each with a role: admin, builder, member or viewer.

The modules

Work by talking

  • Home and AI Chat: ask anything, start anything.
  • Apps Builder: describe an app, get an app.
  • Store: the company's approved apps and IOTx templates.
  • Add new: connect outside apps (accounting, ERP, WhatsApp).

Everyday tools

  • Mail, Calendar, Meetings
  • Docs, Sheets, Slides
  • Files for organizing, Notes
  • Tasks (boards and sprints, like Jira)
  • Read: the company's policies, manuals and SOPs

Who sees what

The admin switches each module and each app on or off for each role and department. A dispatch clerk might see only Home, Tasks and the Dispatch Tracker app. A plant head sees everything for Production. Anything a person can't see simply isn't in their sidebar.

Where an app's data lives

  • IOTx Cloud database: the default, set up automatically when an app needs to save data.
  • The company's own sheets: a Google Sheet or a sheet in IOTx Sheets becomes the app's data.
  • Their own database (for example Supabase), or an existing system connected through "Add new".

How apps are shared

A builder makes an app, then shares it: with named people, a department, a role, or the whole company. Apps the admin approves appear in the Store for everyone allowed to see them. Public-facing apps (a customer order form, for example) can be published to a web address.

Roll-out for a new client

  1. Demo on their own data. Bring one of their sheets and turn it into an app in the meeting.
  2. Set up. Organization, departments, company sign-in, the modules they bought.
  3. Connect what they already use. Mail and calendar, their sheets, their accounting or ERP.
  4. Train two or three builders. One session; they build their first real app with IOTx beside them.
  5. Open it up. Everyone else gets access to the tools and apps meant for them.
  6. Review monthly. Usage, what was built, and what should become a Transformation project.
